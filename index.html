Concept

The meaning behind the name

The name blends “mahal” and “ina”, Tagalog for “love” and “mother”, as a tribute to the country I love and I’m originally from. The website opens on a landing page that introduces the idea, making the visitor choose the postcard best suited for what kind of traveler they are.

Pick your postcard

From there, you pick one of three postcards: the nature adventurer, the city explorer, or a mix of both. Each leads to a 7-day itinerary of the Philippines with different activities and recommendations. Same destination, different story, and an invitation to visit.

Assets

The look and feel of Mahalina

Every visual choice in Mahalina is meant to feel like a postcard from home: warm, personal and easy to read.

Written by hand, sent with love

The Mahalina logo is set in Tersanjung, a script typeface with the flow of real handwriting. It gives the name the feel of a note written to someone you care about, which suits a project that is a tribute to my home country.

Clean structure, clear voice

Stack Sans Headline handles titles and headings, balancing the expressive logo with a modern, confident look. It keeps itineraries and tips easy to scan, so the handwritten touch stays a highlight rather than a distraction.

Colours that follow your journey

Each postcard has its own palette, so the whole experience feels cohesive once you choose a path. The tones also recall the colours of the Philippine flag, tying the design back to the country it celebrates.

Prototypes

Designed for the desk and the pocket

Desktop gives the postcards and itineraries room to breathe, while mobile keeps the same flow with stacked layouts and thumb-friendly interactions.

Room to explore

Generous spacing and strong hierarchy make it comfortable to browse and compare, as if you were spreading the postcards across a table.

Highlights

What makes it special

A few details shape how Mahalina feels from the first postcard to the last tip card. Here are some details:

Your choice shapes everything

  • Three postcards, three personalities: the traveller type shapes the whole itinerary.
  • Color-coded journeys: accent colors follow your choice through every page.
  • Interactive tips cards: quick, bite-sized info, one click away.
  • A personal touch: a handwritten logo and a postcard feel throughout, made as a love letter to home.

From project to case study

This page is also a project.

Mahalina started as a simple wish: to share the country I love in a way that feels personal. This section walks through how that idea became a designed, prototyped website, one stage at a time.

  • I started with the idea itself: one country, three types of travellers. I outlined the postcard choices, planned what each 7 days' itinerary should offer, and defined the tone, which is warm, personal and a little nostalgic.

  • I turned the concept into screens by choosing the typography, building the color palettes, and designing the postcards, itineraries and Tips and Tricks cards. Each page gets its own accent color, and the layouts adapt from desktop to mobile.

  • Finally, I linked the screens into interactive prototypes and tested the flow from landing page to itinerary. I checked readability, spacing and navigation on both desktop and mobile, and refined the details until it all felt consistent.

Case study coding project for the practical exam of ITS Web Design and Digital Strategies 26/27. This page uses Primer Brand's React components.
